## 1:1 — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Ibn Kathir records that the Companions began the Book of Allah with
the Bismillah. He documents disagreement over whether it is a verse of Al-Fatihah
itself: the majority of the Kufan reciters, a group of Companions and Tabi'in held
it is a separate verse at the surah's opening; Malik, Abu Hanifa and their followers
held it is not a verse of Al-Fatihah (or any surah).
Sources:
- https://www.alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/1/0/ (surah introduction;
  quran.com/1:1 shows the same intro text, not verse-specific commentary)
- https://mudahamatan.home.blog/2020/04/17/quran-tafsir-ibn-kathir-surah-1-al-fatihah/
Verification: web excerpts only; page-level citations (volume/page) not confirmed.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Consulted PDF mirror:
https://www.slideshare.net/zakir2012/tafsir-al-jalalayn-english-quran-with-in-depth-commentary —
extraction too unreliable for per-verse notes.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): Tabatabai explains the verse as Allah beginning His speech with His
own name, stamping the chapter's ideas with it, and as a lesson to mankind to begin
all works in Allah's name. The preposition "bi" (in/with) attaches to an implied
verb, "I begin". "Allah" is established as the proper name of God. ar-Rahman denotes
the all-encompassing mercy (believers and unbelievers alike); ar-Rahim the special,
perpetual mercy reserved for believers — supported with a cited tradition of
as-Sadiq. Notably, the commentary states that the Basmalah-is-a-verse position "has
also been narrated by the Sunni narrators" (citing ad-Daraqutni from Abu Hurayrah),
and records Ahl al-Bayt traditions affirming it is part of every chapter except
Surah 9.
Source:
- https://al-islam.org/gu/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-fatiha-verses-1-5
  ("Verse 1" commentary + "Traditions" section)
Verification: web excerpt only; digital text diverges from print per site notice.

**Agreements / Differences**
Evident from excerpts: both sources discuss the Basmalah's status as a verse and the
mercy distinction between ar-Rahman and ar-Rahim. Al-Mizan explicitly acknowledges
Sunni narrations supporting the Basmalah-as-verse view. Finer points of disagreement
not established from these excerpts.

## 1:2 — ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Under "The Meaning of Al-Hamd", Ibn Kathir quotes Abu Ja'far bin
Jarir (al-Tabari): "Alhamdu lillah" means all thanks and praise belong purely to
Allah alone — not to any false object of worship nor any creature — on account of
His innumerable favors and bounties known only to Him, including creating the means
of worship, bodies, and sustenance. He further distinguishes hamd (praise, broader —
for attributes and deeds) from shukr (thanks, for favors received).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/2 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)
Verification: web excerpt only; truncated on page ("…"); full passage not read.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Same PDF-mirror limitation as 1:1.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): Tabatabai distinguishes "al-hamd" (praise for good acquired by one's
own will) from the broader "al-madh". The "al" denotes all praise; since every good
emanates from Allah, all praise in reality belongs to Him. The verse trains the
servant to praise Allah in Allah's own chosen words — citing the tradition "I do not
enumerate Thy praise; Thou art as Thou Thyself hast praised Thyself." Supporting
traditions from as-Sadiq and Imam 'Ali are quoted.
Source:
- https://al-islam.org/gu/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-fatiha-verses-1-5
  ("Verse 2" commentary)
Verification: web excerpt only; digital text diverges from print per site notice.

**Agreements / Differences**
Evident from excerpts: both sources treat "all praise" as exclusively Allah's due and
both analyze the hamd/shukr (or hamd/madh) distinction. No differences established
from these excerpts.

## 1:3 — ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Ibn Kathir notes the two names were already explained under the
Basmalah, then quotes al-Qurtubi: Allah described Himself as ar-Rahman, ar-Rahim
right after "Lord of the worlds", so the statement carries both mercy and a
reminder of accountability (excerpt truncated at this point on the page).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/3 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)

## 1:4 — مَـٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Under "Indicating Sovereignty on the Day of Judgment": Allah
singled out His sovereignty of the Day of Resurrection, but this does not negate His
sovereignty over all other things — He is Lord of this world and the Hereafter; the
Day is singled out because all dominion visibly belongs to Him alone then (excerpt
truncated on page).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/4 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)
Verification: web excerpt only; truncated on page ("…"); full passage not read.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Same PDF-mirror limitation as 1:1.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): Tabatabai discusses the two canonical readings — "malik" (Master /
Owner, from milk/possession) and "malik" (King / Sovereign, from mulk) — holding both
correct of Allah: He is Master as well as King. Linguistically, "King" suits a time
context ("King of that time" is idiomatic; "master of that time" is strained), and
the verse attaches the word to a "day", favoring the King reading — while affirming
both readings valid.
Source:
- https://al-islam.org/gu/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-fatiha-verses-1-5
  ("Verses 2, 3 & 4" commentary)
Verification: web excerpt only; digital text diverges from print per site notice.

**Agreements / Differences**
Not established from these excerpts. (Al-Mizan's qira'at discussion is source-specific
content; whether Ibn Kathir addresses it was not visible in the truncated excerpt —

## 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Defines 'ibadah linguistically as submissiveness ("a road described
as mu'abbadah, paved") and religiously as the utmost love, humility and fear. Notes
the merit of placing the object ("Thee") before the verb — signifying exclusivity of
worship and of seeking help (excerpt truncated on page).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/5 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)
Verification: web excerpt only; truncated on page ("…"); full passage not read.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Same PDF-mirror limitation as 1:1.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): Lengthy analysis: 'ubudiyyah as total servitude before Allah's
unlimited, unshared ownership. The fronted object "Thee" marks exclusivity — worship
is diametrically opposed to arrogance. The shift from third person (previous verses)
to second person reflects the worshipper's presence before the Lord, body and soul.
The plural "we" effaces egotism and self-importance. The second clause ("we beseech
Thee for help") removes any impression of the servant's independence — the servant
owns nothing of himself.
Source:
- https://al-islam.org/gu/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-fatiha-verses-1-5
  ("Verse 5" commentary)
Verification: web excerpt only; digital text diverges from print per site notice.

**Agreements / Differences**
Evident from excerpts: both sources emphasize exclusivity through the fronted object
("Thee") and analyze the linguistic structure of the verse. No differences
established from these excerpts.

## 1:6 — ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): "The Meaning of Guidance mentioned in the Surah": the guidance asked
for means being directed and led to success — the servant asks Allah to guide,
direct, lead, and grant correct guidance (excerpt truncated on page).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/6 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)
Verification: web excerpt only; truncated on page ("…"); full passage not read.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Same PDF-mirror limitation as 1:1.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): as-Sirat is synonymous with at-tariq and as-sabil. The straight path
is the path of those Allah favored; it is the ultimate goal of worship. All creation
proceeds on a prescribed road toward Allah, but there are two ways — the straight
path and another. The straight path is singular (while "ways" are many), free of
polytheism, injustice and straying — true monotheism in belief and deed.
Source:

## 1:7 — صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ

**Sunni — Ibn Kathir**
Summary (draft): Connects the verse to the hadith of the servant's proclamation —
when the servant says "Guide us to the straight way", Allah says, "This is for My
servant, and My servant shall acquire what he asks for." The path is that of those
upon whom Allah bestowed favor (excerpt truncated on page; the classical
identification of the remaining two groups was NOT visible in the excerpt and is not
repeated here).
Source: https://quran.com/al-fatihah/7 ("Ibn Kathir (Abridged)" excerpt)
Verification: web excerpt only; truncated on page ("…"); full passage not read.

**Sunni — Jalalayn**
Not accessible — needs manual verification. (Same PDF-mirror limitation as 1:1.)

**Shia — Al-Mizan**
Summary (draft): Reads the verse as naming three categories: those proceeding upward
(believers, the favored), those falling to lower levels (those under wrath), and the
astray. The favored are identified through 4:69 — prophets, the truthful, martyrs,
the righteous. Straying, polytheism and injustice are treated as one in effect
(citing 2:108, 6:82). The straight path is distinct from the latter two paths.
Source:
- https://al-islam.org/hi/al-mizan-exegesis-quran-volume-1-sayyid-muhammad-husayn-tabatabai/surah-al-fatiha-verses-6-7
  ("Verses 6–7" commentary)
Verification: web excerpt only; digital text diverges from print per site notice.

**Agreements / Differences**

comparable categorization; nothing assumed).
