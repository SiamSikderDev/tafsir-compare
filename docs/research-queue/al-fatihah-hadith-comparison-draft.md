# Al-Fatihah (1:1–1:7) — Hadith & Comparison Research Draft

> **STATUS: DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLISHING.**
> Companion research to `al-fatihah-draft.md` (tafsir). Contains hadith
> references and per-verse Sunni/Shia comparison notes, prepared for human
> review. Nothing here may be published, quoted as final religious knowledge,
> or entered into the database as `published` without explicit reviewer
> approval, per `docs/research-methodology.md` §7.

- **Scope:** Surah Al-Fatihah, verses 1–7.
- **Method:** Hadith references verified against published secondary sources
  (abuaminaelias.com, imamghazali.org, dorar.net, onepathnetwork.com,
  al-islam.org). Collection + number given only where sources agree; where
  numbering is uncertain it is marked "needs verification". Meanings are
  summarized in the researcher's own words — NOT direct quotations.
- **Comparisons** are derived from `al-fatihah-draft.md` only. Agreements and
  differences are stated ONLY where the tafsir draft supports them.

## Hadiths

### H1 — The servant's proclamation (Hadith Qudsi)

- **Collection:** Sahih Muslim, **395** (sunnah.com numbering; some editions
  number it differently — e.g. Kitab as-Salah variants)
- **Narrator:** Abu Hurayrah
- **Grading:** Sahih
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7); especially 1:5–1:7
- **Summary (EN):** Allah says He has divided the prayer between Himself and
  His servant into two halves, and the servant shall have what he asks for.
  Each part of Al-Fatihah draws a divine response: praise, extolment,
  glorification, the shared covenant of worship and help-seeking, and the
  granting of guidance.
- **Summary (BN):** আল্লাহ বলেছেন, তিনি সালাতকে নিজের ও বান্দার মধ্যে দুই
  ভাগে ভাগ করেছেন; বান্দা যা চাইবে তা পাবে। ফাতিহার প্রতিটি অংশের জবাবে
  আল্লাহ সাড়া দেন — প্রশংসা, মহিমা ঘোষণা, ইবাদত ও সাহায্য প্রার্থনার অঙ্গীকার,
  এবং হেদায়েত দান।
- **Relevance:** Core hadith for the surah's status as a living dialogue in
  prayer; directly cited in the Ibn Kathir excerpt for 1:7.
- **Source URL:** https://en.tohed.com/threads/40-authentic-hadiths-on-virtues-of-islamic-deeds.11216/

### H2 — The greatest surah in the Quran

- **Collection:** Sahih al-Bukhari, **5006** (sunnah.com; variant numbers in
  older prints: 4474)
- **Narrator:** Abu Sa'id ar-Rafi' ibn al-Mu'alla
- **Grading:** Sahih
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7)
- **Summary (EN):** The Prophet ﷺ promised to teach the greatest surah in the
  Quran before leaving the mosque, then identified it as "Alhamdu lillahi
  Rabbil-'alamin" — the seven oft-repeated verses (as-sab' al-mathani) and the
  Great Quran given to him.
- **Summary (BN):** রাসূল ﷺ মসজিদ ত্যাগের আগে কুরআনের সর্বশ্রেষ্ঠ সূরা
  শেখানোর প্রতিশ্রুতি দিয়ে সূরা আল-ফাতিহাকে চিহ্নিত করেন — সাতটি বারবার
  পঠিত আয়াত (আস-সাব'উল-মাসানী) এবং তাঁকে প্রদত্ত মহান কুরআন।
- **Relevance:** Establishes the surah's rank; basis for the name as-Sab'
  al-Mathani (cf. 15:87).
- **Source URL:** https://www.abuaminaelias.com/al-mundhiri-surah-al-fatihah/

### H3 — Al-Fatihah as ruqyah (healing)

- **Collection:** Sahih al-Bukhari, **5736**
- **Narrator:** Abu Sa'id al-Khudri
- **Grading:** Sahih
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7)
- **Summary (EN):** A companion recited Al-Fatihah over a tribe's chief who
  had been stung/bitten, and the man was cured. When asked about it, the
  Prophet ﷺ said, "How did you know that it is a ruqyah?" and permitted them
  the payment they had received.
- **Summary (BN):** এক সাহাবী সূরা আল-ফাতিহা পড়ে এক গোত্রপ্রধানের চিকিৎসা
  করেন এবং তিনি সুস্থ হন। রাসূল ﷺ জিজ্ঞেস করেন, "তুমি কীভাবে জানলে এটি
  রুকইয়া?" এবং প্রাপ্ত পারিশ্রমিক অনুমোদন করেন।
- **Relevance:** Basis for the surah's names ash-Shafiyah (the Healing) and
  al-Kafiyah (the Sufficing).
- **Source URL:** https://sidrayasin.substack.com/p/seeking-cure-from-the-nature-pt-1

### H4 — No prayer without the Opening of the Book

- **Collection:** Sahih al-Bukhari, **756**
- **Narrator:** 'Ubadah ibn as-Samit
- **Grading:** Sahih
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7)
- **Summary (EN):** "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the
  Opening of the Book." Establishes reciting Al-Fatihah as an essential part
  of every rak'ah of prayer.
- **Summary (BN):** "যে ব্যক্তি 'ফাতিহাতুল কিতাব' পড়ে না, তার সালাত হয় না।"
  প্রত্যেক রাকাতে ফাতিহা পড়া সালাতের অপরিহার্য অংশ হিসেবে প্রতিষ্ঠিত।
- **Relevance:** Legal basis for the surah's obligatory recitation in prayer.
- **Source URL:** https://dorar.net/en/ahadith?page=28&activeTab=panel-2&muslim-page=37

### H5 — The two lights

- **Collection:** Sahih Muslim, **806** (confirmed by abuaminaelias.com and
  imamghazali.org; sunnah.com numbering)
- **Narrator:** Ibn 'Abbas
- **Grading:** Sahih
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7)
- **Summary (EN):** An angel descended from a newly opened door of heaven and
  gave glad tidings of two lights granted to no prophet before: the Opening
  of the Book (Al-Fatihah) and the last verses of al-Baqarah — no letter of
  them is recited except its benefit is granted.
- **Summary (BN):** আকাশের নব-উন্মোচিত দরজা দিয়ে এক ফেরেশতা নেমে দুটি নূরের
  সুসংবাদ দেন, যা পূর্ববর্তী কোনো নবীকে দেওয়া হয়নি: ফাতিহাতুল কিতাব ও সূরা
  বাকারার শেষ আয়াতগুলো — এর কোনো অক্ষর পড়লেই তার কল্যাণ দেওয়া হয়।
- **Relevance:** Virtue/excellence (fada'il) of the surah.
- **Source URL:** https://www.abuaminaelias.com/dailyhadithonline/2021/04/27/fatihah-baqarah-descend/

### H6 — Nothing like it revealed before (Ubayy ibn Ka'b)

- **Collection:** Sunan at-Tirmidhi, **2875**; also Musnad Ahmad, 2/357
- **Narrator:** Ubayy ibn Ka'b
- **Grading:** Hasan Sahih (at-Tirmidhi)
- **Related verses:** Whole surah (1:1–1:7)
- **Summary (EN):** The Prophet ﷺ asked Ubayy what he recites in prayer; he
  recited Al-Fatihah. The Prophet ﷺ said nothing like it was revealed in the
  Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or the Furqan — it is the seven oft-repeated verses
  and the mighty Quran given to him.
- **Summary (BN):** রাসূল ﷺ উবাইকে জিজ্ঞেস করেন সালাতে কী পড়েন; তিনি ফাতিহা
  পড়েন। রাসূল ﷺ বলেন, তাওরাত, ইনজিল, যাবুর বা ফুরকানে এর মতো কিছু নাজিল
  হয়নি — এটি সাতটি বারবার পঠিত আয়াত ও তাঁকে প্রদত্ত মহান কুরআন।
- **Relevance:** Virtue/excellence of the surah.
- **Source URL:** https://en.tohed.com/threads/the-unmatched-virtues-of-surah-al-fatihah-in-authentic-hadith.11489/

## Per-verse comparisons

Derived from `al-fatihah-draft.md`. Agreements/differences only where the
draft supports them.

### 1:1 — Basmalah

- **sunniView:** Ibn Kathir records that the Companions began the Book of
  Allah with the Bismillah, and documents disagreement over whether it is a
  verse of Al-Fatihah itself: the majority of the Kufan reciters and a group
  of Companions and Tabi'in held it is a separate verse at the surah's
  opening, while Malik, Abu Hanifa and their followers held it is not a
  verse of Al-Fatihah.
- **shiaView:** Tabatabai explains the verse as Allah beginning His speech
  with His own name — a lesson for mankind to begin all works in Allah's
  name. The "bi" attaches to an implied verb ("I begin"); "Allah" is the
  proper name of God; ar-Rahman is universal mercy (believers and unbelievers
  alike), ar-Rahim the special, perpetual mercy for believers. Ahl al-Bayt
  traditions affirm it is part of every chapter except Surah 9.
- **comparison:** Both treat the verse as the Book's opening formula and both
  address its verse-status. Notably, Al-Mizan itself acknowledges Sunni
  narrations (ad-Daraqutni from Abu Hurayrah) supporting the
  Basmalah-as-verse position, so the affirmative position is shared across
  the schools; the recorded Sunni disagreement (Malik/Abu Hanifa vs. the
  Kufans) is intra-Sunni. Both distinguish the two mercy names along
  universal/special lines.
- **agreements:** (1) The Basmalah opens the Book. (2) The universal/special
  mercy distinction between ar-Rahman and ar-Rahim. (3) Sunni narrations
  supporting the Basmalah-as-verse view are acknowledged by both sides.
- **differences:** Finer points of disagreement not established from these
  excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) "bi" attaches to an implied verb — "I begin". (2)
  "Allah" established as the proper name (ism 'alam) of God.

### 1:2 — Al-hamdu lillahi rabbil-'alamin

- **sunniView:** Via al-Tabari, "Alhamdu lillah" means all thanks and praise
  belong purely to Allah alone — not to any false object of worship nor any
  creature — for His innumerable favors known only to Him. Hamd (praise,
  broader — for attributes and deeds) is distinguished from shukr (thanks,
  for favors received).
- **shiaView:** Al-hamd (praise for good acquired by one's own will) is
  distinguished from the broader al-madh. The "al" denotes all praise; since
  every good emanates from Allah, all praise in reality belongs to Him. The
  verse trains the servant to praise Allah in Allah's own chosen words.
- **comparison:** Strong convergence: both hold that all praise belongs
  exclusively to Allah, and both perform the same analytical move of
  distinguishing hamd from a neighboring term — shukr in the Sunni excerpt,
  madh in the Shia excerpt. The underlying point (praise's scope and proper
  object) is identical.
- **agreements:** (1) All praise exclusively Allah's due. (2) The hamd
  distinction analysis.
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) hamd vs shukr vs madh. (2) The "al" of
  comprehensiveness (istighraq).

### 1:3 — Ar-Rahmanir-Rahim

- **sunniView:** The two names were explained under the Basmalah; quoting
  al-Qurtubi, Allah described Himself as ar-Rahman, ar-Rahim immediately
  after "Lord of the worlds", so the statement carries both mercy and a
  reminder of accountability.
- **shiaView:** (Within the 1:1 commentary) ar-Rahman's mercy is universal —
  for believers and unbelievers in this world; ar-Rahim's mercy is reserved
  for believers and lasts into the hereafter. Cited from as-Sadiq: "ar-Rahman
  is a special name with a general attribute; ar-Rahim is a general name with
  a special attribute."
- **comparison:** Both read the two names as complementary aspects of mercy
  forming a two-tier structure. The Sunni excerpt adds the pairing of mercy
  with accountability (placed after "Lord of the worlds"); the Shia excerpt
  adds the worldly/hereafter scope distinction. The core two-tier mercy
  reading is shared.
- **agreements:** (1) Two-tier mercy (universal vs. special).
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) as-Sadiq's name/attribute formulation (special
  name–general attribute vs. general name–special attribute).

### 1:4 — Maliki yawmid-din

- **sunniView:** Singling out Allah's sovereignty of the Day of Resurrection
  does not negate His sovereignty over all else — He is Lord of this world
  and the Hereafter. The Day is singled out because all dominion visibly
  belongs to Him alone then.
- **shiaView:** The two canonical readings — "malik" (Master/Owner, from
  milk) and "malik" (King/Sovereign, from mulk) — are both correct of Allah:
  He is Master as well as King. Since the word attaches to a "day", the King
  reading is favored linguistically, while both readings remain valid.
- **comparison:** The excerpts address different facets rather than opposing
  claims: the Sunni excerpt gives the theological rationale for singling out
  the Day; the Shia excerpt addresses the qira'at question. No contradiction
  is evident; the two angles are complementary.
- **agreements:** Not established from these excerpts (different facets
  addressed).
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) malik vs malik readings; milk (possession) vs mulk
  (sovereignty) roots. (2) "King of that time" is idiomatic; "master of that
  time" is strained — favoring the King reading in a time context.

### 1:5 — Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in

- **sunniView:** 'Ibadah linguistically means submissiveness and religiously
  the utmost love, humility and fear. Placing the object ("Thee") before the
  verb signifies exclusivity — of worship and of seeking help.
- **shiaView:** 'Ubudiyyah is total servitude before Allah's unlimited,
  unshared ownership. The fronted object "Thee" marks exclusivity. The shift
  from third person to second person reflects the worshipper's presence
  before the Lord; the plural "we" effaces egotism; the second clause removes
  any impression of the servant's independence.
- **comparison:** Strong convergence: both center exclusivity through the
  fronted object and both analyze the verse's grammar. Al-Mizan extends the
  analysis further (person shift, the plural, ownership metaphysics), but
  nothing in the excerpts contradicts the Sunni reading.
- **agreements:** (1) Exclusivity through the fronted object ("Thee"). (2)
  Grammatical/linguistic analysis of the verse structure.
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) Fronting (taqdim) of the object for exclusivity.
  (2) 'Ibadah from submissiveness (a road described as mu'abbadah, paved).
  (3) Third-to-second person shift marking presence.

### 1:6 — Ihdinas-siratal-mustaqim

- **sunniView:** The guidance asked for means being directed and led to
  success — the servant asks Allah to guide, direct, lead, and grant correct
  guidance.
- **shiaView:** As-sirat is synonymous with the road/path. The straight path
  is the path of those Allah favored — the ultimate goal of worship. The
  straight path is singular (while "ways" are many), free of polytheism,
  injustice and straying: true monotheism in belief and deed.
- **comparison:** The shared core is the request for divine guidance. The
  Shia excerpt develops the path's singularity and its monotheistic content
  more fully; the Sunni excerpt (truncated) stays with the meaning of the
  guidance requested. No disagreement is evident.
- **agreements:** (1) The guidance theme.
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts.
- **linguisticNotes:** (1) sirat/tariq/sabil synonymy. (2) Singular "path"
  vs. plural "ways".

### 1:7 — Siratal-ladhina an'amta 'alayhim...

- **sunniView:** Connected to the servant-proclamation hadith: when the
  servant says "Guide us to the straight way", Allah says, "This is for My
  servant, and My servant shall acquire what he asks for." The path is that
  of those upon whom Allah bestowed favor. (The excerpt was truncated before
  any categorization of the remaining two groups.)
- **shiaView:** Three categories are named: those proceeding upward (the
  favored believers), those under wrath, and the astray. The favored are
  identified through 4:69 — prophets, the truthful, martyrs, the righteous.
  Straying, polytheism and injustice are treated as one in effect.
- **comparison:** Both identify the favored group positively as those Allah
  blessed. The Shia excerpt gives an explicit three-category mapping with a
  Qur'anic cross-reference (4:69); whether the Sunni sources categorize the
  remaining groups the same way was not visible in the truncated excerpt and
  is not assumed.
- **agreements:** (1) The path is that of those Allah favored.
- **differences:** Not established from these excerpts (Sunni excerpt
  truncated before comparable categorization).
- **linguisticNotes:** None established from these excerpts. (Related
  practice: saying "Ameen" after 1:7 — see H-adjacent hadith, Sahih
  al-Bukhari 4150, cited in secondary literature; primary verification
  pending, not included as a numbered entry.)
